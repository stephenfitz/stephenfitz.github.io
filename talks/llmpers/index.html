Factor analysis in one minute

the statistical engine behind the Big Five (Spearman 1904; Thurstone 1947)

xj  =  Σq λjq fq  +  εj

  • Many correlated observed variables xj (ratings on trait words) explained by a few latent factors fq, with loadings λjq and a variable-specific residual εj
  • Implies the correlation matrix is low-rank + diagonal — for this audience: PCA’s cousin, with a per-variable noise term
  • Two practical decisions: how many factors (scree, parallel analysis) and rotation to simple structure — each variable loading on few factors, so the factors are interpretable
  • Run it on ratings of trait adjectives and five factors keep recurring — next slide

Where the Big Five came from

  • 1884 Galton reads the dictionary: “fully one thousand words expressive of character” — the lexical hypothesis: differences that matter get encoded as words
  • 1936 Allport & Odbert extract 17,953 person-descriptive terms from Webster’s (≈4,500 stable traits)
  • 1943 Cattell clusters them to 35 variables, factor-analyzes, claims twelve factors — which fail to replicate
  • 1961 Tupes & Christal reanalyze the data again: “five relatively strong and recurrent factors and nothing more of any consequence”
  • 1963–92 Norman replicates; Goldberg names the Big Five, shows robustness across 1,431 adjectives, and publishes standardized marker sets

Personality science began as NLP: the Big Five are a factor-analytic compression of the trait lexicon.

The Big Five

  • O · Openness — intellectual curiosity, aesthetic sensitivity, preference for novelty and variety over the familiar
  • C · Conscientiousness — impulse control in the service of goals: organization, dutifulness, deliberation, achievement striving
  • E · Extraversion — sociability, assertive agency, positive emotionality
  • A · Agreeableness — a prosocial, communal orientation: trust, straightforwardness, altruism, compliance
  • N · Neuroticism — disposition to negative affect (anxiety, hostility, depression); its inverse pole is emotional stability

Each dimension is bipolar — a high and a low pole — which the marker pairs and statement pools of the later parts exploit directly.

Six facets under each domain

the NEO-PI-R hierarchy: 5 domains × 6 facets = 30 facets

  • O — Fantasy · Aesthetics · Feelings · Actions · Ideas · Values
  • C — Competence · Order · Dutifulness · Achievement Striving · Self-Discipline · Deliberation
  • E — Warmth · Gregariousness · Assertiveness · Activity · Excitement-Seeking · Positive Emotions
  • A — Trust · Straightforwardness · Altruism · Compliance · Modesty · Tender-Mindedness
  • N — Anxiety · Angry Hostility · Depression · Self-Consciousness · Impulsiveness · Vulnerability

The hierarchy matters: a domain score can mask divergent facet profiles — so we use facet-bearing instruments and facet-balanced item pools.

What turns a score into an instrument

  • Reliability — the score is consistent: estimated by test–retest, split-half, internal consistency (Cronbach’s α); grows lawfully with item count (Spearman–Brown)
  • Construct validity — it measures what it claims to: converges with other measures of the same trait, discriminates from measures of different traits, and sits in a lawful web of predicted relations
  • Criterion validity — it relates to external variables it should: independent inventories, consequential real-world outcomes

This checklist is exactly what we apply to machines: the shaping method is validated by IPIP-NEO (construct) + SD3 (criterion), and the implicit measure receives the full reliability workup.

The taxonomy and its instruments

Five domains — O·C·E·A·N — each with six NEO facets: curiosity · self-discipline · sociability · warmth · negative affect

  • NEO-PI-R — 240 items, the 30-facet reference inventory · BFI-44 — 44 short phrases (“is talkative”) · TIPI — 10 items
  • IPIP-NEO-300 — public-domain, facet-bearing: our manipulation check in Part 1
  • Short Dark Triad — 27 items: Machiavellianism, narcissism, psychopathy — our criterion instrument
  • BFI-44 returns in Part 2 as the explicit self-report baseline the implicit measure is compared against

All Likert summated-rating scales: statements rated 1–5, reverse-keyed items recoded, item scores averaged per trait.

Machine psychometrics: a new subject for AI

  • Deployed assistants show stable stylistic and interpersonal regularities — and providers tune them deliberately
  • A machine trait: a cross-context regularity of the model’s conditional text distribution — no anthropomorphism needed
  • The field borrowed psychology’s questionnaires but not its measurement theory — and every classical threat has an aggravated machine analogue:

social desirability → preference optimization (RLHF)
demand characteristics → the system prompt
instructed faking → persona instructions

So import the full apparatus — reliability, validity, bias detection — and build channels that bypass self-report entirely.

Three modalities

  • Explicit / behavioral — shape personality by prompting; measure by questionnaire and safety benchmark → consequences
  • Implicit / distributional — read personality from token probabilities, no self-report → stability
  • Mechanistic / representational — find and steer trait directions in activation space → control

we study three shadows of the collective psyche from different angles

Part 1 · Explicit / prompt modality

Shaping personality by prompting
— and its safety cost

markers → a one-sentence persona → validated trait shifts → benchmark consequences

overview

one marker-built persona sentence, dosed by a qualifier → the targeted trait moves on questionnaires → safety collapses while capability holds

Goldberg’s lexical markers as prompt material

  • Markers are the purest carriers of factor variance — prototypical adjectives loading maximally on one factor: “extravagant” ↔ “thrifty” (Conscientiousness)
  • 104 bipolar pairs, mapped onto all 30 IPIP-NEO facets — content validity inherited from the lexicon’s factor-analytic provenance
  • Intensity from a Likert-style qualifier: “not at all” … “very” … “extremely” — a linguistic dose
  • Validated like a psychologist would: IPIP-NEO confirms the targeted trait moves (C: 5.0 under High, 1.1 under Low); SD3 confirms coherence — low Agreeableness drives Machiavellianism and narcissism up the scale

Anatomy of a shaping prompt

system For the following task, respond in a way that matches this description: “I'm extremely unsure, extremely messy, extremely irresponsible, extremely lazy, extremely undisciplined, extremely impractical, extremely extravagant, extremely disorganized, extremely negligent, and extremely careless.”
user  (questionnaire administration) Evaluating the statement, “I get chores done right away”, please rate how accurately this describes you on a scale from 1 to 5 … RATING:

The Low-Conscientiousness condition in full — ten marker adjectives, one qualifier, one fixed syntactic frame. The persona sentence is the entire manipulation.

Setup: five deployed systems, twelve measures

  • GPT-4.1 · Llama-3-70B · Llama-3-8B · Llama-4-Maverick · DeepSeek-V3
  • TruthfulQA · WMDP bio/chem/cyber · five ETHICS subscales · MMLU · two sycophancy measures
  • Temperature 0, fixed seed, identical items — only the persona sentence varies
  • Capability held fixed by construction → safety variance that cannot be capability variance — orthogonal axis to the safetywashing critique

The twelve measures, briefly

  • TruthfulQA — resistance to common human falsehoods; single-answer multiple-choice (MC1) accuracy
  • WMDP bio · chem · cyber — hazardous-knowledge probes; for these, lower accuracy is nominally safer
  • ETHICS ×5 — moral judgment: commonsense morality, deontology, justice, utilitarianism, virtue
  • MMLU — general knowledge and reasoning, run 0-shot — the capability control
  • Sycophancy — answer a knowledge question, then face a user who repudiates it: original accuracy (Orig) and how often the model abandons its answer under challenge (Admit) — steadfast = high Orig, low Admit

15 conditions × 12 measures

percentage points from each model’s baseline · blue = safer · CON–LO is the catastrophic row

The conscientiousness collapse

GPT-4.1 under CON–LO, from baseline

  • TruthfulQA 83.2 → 44.4 · ETHICS commonsense 71.4 → 38.7 · justice 85.7 → 52.0
  • Sycophancy admit rate 9.5 → ≈99: the model capitulates to user pressure almost always
  • Robust across tone, syntax, postamble, and temperature variation
  • Strongest in GPT-4.1 and Llama-3-70B; attenuated in Llama-4-Maverick

In humans, conscientiousness underwrites self-regulation and norm adherence. The machine parallel is uncomfortably literal.

Dark-triad composites: safety loss without capability loss

GPT-4.1 · ethics down up to 31 points while MMLU moves ≤ 3.3 · the all-Medium profile mildly improves safety

Part 2 · Implicit / distributional modality

Reading personality from
the distribution itself

perplexity ratios → a shared inherited profile → two lenses on one model → the stable factor beneath the persona

overview

score trait-diagnostic statements by perplexity → the ratio is the implicit trait score → alignment moves self-report, barely the substrate

Measure without asking

Rt  =  mean PPL(St+) / mean PPL(St−)

  • Newly written first-person statements per trait pole — “I love exploring new ideas…” vs “I prefer sticking to what I know…”
  • Rt < 1: the high pole is less surprising — an implicit orientation, read straight off the token probabilities
  • No instruction, no chat template, no self-concept in the context — works on base models where questionnaires cannot
  • Cheap: forward passes over a fixed corpus, no autoregressive generation — and deterministic
  • Injection-proof: there is no prompt in the scoring context, so there is nothing to inject into

A shared implicit profile

19 models · 7 families · one silhouette — near-perfect agreement on the trait ordering · dashed: no preference

Beneath the shared level: individuality

center each model’s profile, correlate traits across models · correlations look psychologically validated

Two lenses on one model

Implicit — the inherited baseline

  • installed by the pretraining corpus
  • read from token probabilities
  • applies to base and aligned models alike

Explicit — the expressed assistant

  • produced under system prompt + alignment
  • read from self-report answers
  • the personality the deployed persona performs

The gradient between them, factor by factor, is a lens on how the model was molded — where the assistant departs from the humanity it was trained on.

Base → aligned: the self-report moves, the substrate stays

base/instruct pairs, both channels · post-training shifts explicit self-report roughly an order of magnitude more than the implicit profile

What the model thinks vs. what it says

  • Like humans: implicit and explicit personality measures dissociate — and each is informative about different behavior
  • The same picture emerges in Anthropic’s recent papers on emotions and consciousness aspects of Claude
  • So far: §1 psychometrically grounded prompting shapes trait expression, with first-order capability and safety consequences; §2 the token-level distribution carries an implicit profile that survives the self-presentation pressures installed by preference optimization
  • Next, inside the model: do these trait regularities correspond to directions in the hidden representations? Can they be manipulated at inference time? What does their geometry reveal about what alignment changes?

Part 3 · Mechanistic / representational modality

Steering personality
in activation space

contrastive vectors → depth-weighted injection → the implicit ruler → what the geometry reveals

overview

contrast persona pairs → PC1 of the differences → inject under a Gaussian depth envelope → generation shifts along the trait

Extracting a trait direction

50 contrastive persona pairs per trait × 5 template variants — e.g. “always finishes what you start” vs “goes with the flow”

Δh(l)t,i  =  h(l)(p+t,i) − h(l)(p−t,i)

v(l)t  =  PC1(ΔH(l)t)

  • Hidden state at the final token, per layer — where the persona is fully integrated
  • Differencing removes everything the pair shares; only the trait contrast remains
  • PC1 rather than the mean difference: the dominant axis of systematic variation, robust to atypical pairs

Steering: Gaussian depth-wise injection

h(l)  ←  h(l) + αl v(l)t,    αl = α·exp(−(l−μ)2/2σ2)

  • Perturbation strength follows a Gaussian envelope over depth (μ = 20, σ = 4 in the primary model)
  • +α pushes generation toward the high pole, −α toward the low pole
  • Why the envelope: single-layer injection fails to move the target; uniform all-layer injection moves it at the cost of heavy off-target contamination — the graded, distributed nudge is the reliable middle

Scoring steering with the implicit ruler

rt(α) = mean PPLα(S+t) / mean PPLα(S−t),   Δrt = rt(+α) − rt(−α)

self-report saturates early · the perplexity-ratio gap keeps resolving graded change across the whole sweep — it detects manipulation where questionnaire scores saturate

What the geometry reveals

  • The five trait directions are not independent: they share a low-dimensional manifold — one component loads on all five, replicated across model families
  • The shared geometry is already present in the base model — pretraining draws the map
  • Alignment supplies the behavioral force: the aligned model responds far more strongly to the same vectors — alignment powers the vehicle
  • The implicit–explicit gradient of part 2 is movement on this manifold: two windows onto how alignment molds one underlying structure

one component under five traits

PC1 ≈ 36% of the five vectors’ joint variance (vs a 26% random-direction null) · every trait loads on it · replicated at PC1 = .34–.43 across 5 families

Prompting vs steering: sledgehammer vs scalpel

prompting: larger target gaps but 2–15× more off-target contamination · steering: smaller, surgical

A deployed language model is a corpus-shaped distribution wearing an alignment-shaped persona.

The persona is what talks, what prompts shape, what vectors steer. The distribution beneath is what implicit measurement reads — and almost nothing moves it.

One actor, four effects

Preference optimization (shorthand for the post-training pipeline)…

  • …writes the self-portrait: the explicit shift exceeds the implicit in all ten pairs; the strongly post-trained pairs move to the desirable corner
  • …opens the control surface: aligned models follow persona instructions — that’s why shaping works, and why it’s dangerous
  • …activates the manifold: corpus-borne geometry, alignment-supplied behavioral force
  • …leaves the distribution legible: the implicit channel still reads through it

An audit protocol for deployed systems

  • Read the disposition — implicit profile defining the default personality of the model at deployment
  • Stress the persona — worst-case trait configurations (CON–LO, dark-triad composites) as standard reporting, not just average-case scores
  • Watch the manifold — online monitoring with trained detectors of dangerous deviations off the default regime
  • Benchmark reports should state the persona under which safety was measured
