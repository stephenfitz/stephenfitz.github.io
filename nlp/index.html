Historical Foundations of Computing, AI, and Natural Language Processing
A comprehensive historical overview tracing the evolution from ancient algorithms to modern NLP. Covers the development of computing (from Babylonian mathematics to personal computers), the history of artificial intelligence (from the Dartmouth Conference to deep learning), and the foundations of linguistics and computational language processing (from historical linguistics to transformer architectures).

On Science
Examines what distinguishes a scientific discipline by shifting the focus from the "scientific method" to the nature of scientific explanation, drawing on Carl Hempel's nomological–deductive model and his notion of explanation sketches. Situates artificial intelligence as a science of substrate-free intellectual processes that combines deductive theoretical results with largely statistical explanatory practices.

Matrix Computations
Builds up the matrix product from first principles, develops its geometric interpretation as a composition of linear maps, and connects the theory to practice by implementing it in Python at progressively higher levels of abstraction—from triple nested loops to BLAS—measuring nearly three orders of magnitude in performance difference along the way.

Tensor Derivatives
A practical introduction to tensor calculus as it appears in deep learning. Walks through derivatives of vectors, matrices, and higher-order tensors by reducing each problem to derivatives of individual scalar components, covering Jacobians, matrix–vector products, and the chain rule in a way that maps directly to working code.

Information Theory and Statistics
Introduces fundamental concepts from probability theory, information theory, and statistics that underpin modern machine learning and NLP. Covers random variables, Bayesian modeling, maximum likelihood estimation, entropy, cross-entropy, KL divergence, perplexity, precision/recall/F-measure, and statistical significance testing.
