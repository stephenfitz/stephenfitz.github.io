On Science

What makes a discipline a science?

Overview

  • The "scientific method" is not enough to define science
  • Scientific explanation, not method, is what matters
  • Hempel's nomological-deductive model
  • What differentiates scientific disciplines
  • AI as a scientific discipline

The Scientific Method Is Not Enough

  • Many activities follow something resembling the scientific method:
    • Market research, quality control, A/B testing, engineering trial-and-error
  • These are methodologically "scientific" but remain pragmatic and instrumental
  • The central question has never been just how we investigate
  • It is what kind of explanations we produce

From Method to Explanation

What distinguishes science is not primarily a method, but the kind of explanations it aims to deliver.

  • A discipline is scientific if it attempts to provide scientific explanations of a class of phenomena
  • But what counts as a scientific explanation?

Hempel's Nomological-Deductive Model

  • Carl Hempel: central figure in 20th-century philosophy of science
  • Nomological comes from Greek nomos, meaning law
  • In its ideal form, a scientific explanation has:
    • One or more universal laws
    • Initial or boundary conditions
    • The phenomenon deduced as a logical consequence

The Ideal: Deduction from Laws

An explanation is scientific in nature if it frames an observation under question as an instance of a universal law.

  • Classic examples from physics:
    • Kepler's laws and Newtonian mechanics explain planetary motion
    • Gas laws explain macroscopic thermodynamic behavior
    • Maxwell's equations explain electromagnetic phenomena
  • Explanation is tightly linked to deduction from general laws

Explanation Sketches

  • Most successful sciences do not operate in the idealized nomological-deductive way
  • In biology, psychology, medicine, economics, we rely on:
    • Statistical regularities
    • Probabilistic models
    • Empirical generalizations
  • These provide likelihoods, tendencies, and patterns --- not certainty
  • Hempel called these explanation sketches

Science Without Universal Laws

Being a science does not require possessing universal laws. It requires systematically searching for lawful structure in a domain.

  • Explanation sketches are explanatory in practice
  • They fall short of full law-based deduction
  • But the systematic search for lawful structure is what matters

What Differentiates Scientific Disciplines?

Three dimensions distinguish scientific fields:

  1. The class of phenomena they attempt to explain
  2. The tools they use to search for laws or regularities

  3. The language in which explanations are expressed

  4. Physics, chemistry, biology differ not just in subject matter but in:

    • What counts as an admissible observation
    • What kinds of abstractions are allowed
    • What formal languages describe explanations

AI as a Scientific Discipline

  • Parts of AI produce genuinely nomological-deductive explanations:
    • Convergence theorems in optimization
    • Generalization bounds in learning theory
    • Computational complexity results
    • Formal properties of algorithms
  • Much of modern AI relies on statistical explanation sketches:
    • Empirical scaling laws
    • Performance trends in large models
    • Observed regularities in representation learning

AI Straddles Both Sides

  • AI produces both deductive results and statistical explanation sketches
  • This places AI in the same category as biology, medicine, and psychology
  • Sciences that are in the process of discovering their laws
  • Not a defect --- a characteristic shared by many mature sciences

The Phenomena of AI: Substrate-Free Intelligence

  • AI is concerned with substrate-free cognitive processes:
    • Reasoning, learning, perception, memory
    • Planning, language, decision-making
  • Studied independently of any particular biological or physical implementation
  • Physics abstracts across instances of the same kind of physical system
  • AI abstracts in a different direction: intelligence as an organizational property

Substrate Independence

  • The same functional patterns can be realized across very different substrates
    • Brains, silicon chips, cellular automata
  • AI targets behaviors that are multiply realizable
  • Physics seeks substrate-specific laws of nature
  • AI seeks substrate-independent laws of organization (computation + representation)

The Language of Explanation in AI

AI's explanatory language is a hybrid drawn from several mature sciences:

  • Geometry and Topology: high-dimensional vector spaces, manifolds, embeddings
  • Information Theory and Statistics: entropy, mutual information, Bayesian inference
  • Tensor Computation and Linear Algebra: multilinear maps, spectral properties, backpropagation

Geometry and Topology of Representations

  • High-dimensional real vector spaces
  • Submanifolds induced by learned representations
  • Linear separability and curvature
  • Topological and geometric structure of embeddings
  • This geometric viewpoint underlies modern representation learning

Information Theory and Statistics

  • Entropy, mutual information, likelihood
  • Generalization bounds
  • Bayesian inference
  • Probabilistic modeling
  • These provide the dominant framework for learning and uncertainty

Tensor Computation and Linear Algebra

  • Multilinear maps
  • Spectral properties
  • Optimization in parameter space
  • Backpropagation as structured tensor calculus
  • This is the computational substrate of modern deep learning

Conclusion: Science Without Laws?

  • AI is a science not because it follows a method, but because:
    • It targets a well-defined class of phenomena
    • It systematically searches for lawful structure
    • It expresses its explanations in formal, cumulative languages
  • Whether AI will discover deep, universal laws of intelligence remains open
  • But the scientific character of the enterprise is already clear

Summary

  • Science is defined by the kind of explanations it produces, not just its methods
  • Hempel's model: explanation = deduction from laws + initial conditions
  • Explanation sketches (statistical, partial) are legitimate scientific practice
  • Disciplines differ by phenomena, tools, and explanatory language
  • AI studies substrate-free intelligence using geometry, information theory, and algebra
  • AI is scientific: it systematically searches for the laws of intelligence
